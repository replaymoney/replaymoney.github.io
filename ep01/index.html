How we built the 401(k) simulation

The model behind "If the AI Bubble Pops, What Happens to YOUR 401(k)?" Every assumption, every result, every stress test, and the code to rerun it yourself.

This is an illustration, not a forecast. The simulation can't tell you what the market will do. It shows how the same future plays out for people who make different choices. Education only, not financial advice. Updated Nov 13, 2026.

The dollar amounts depend heavily on our assumptions. The comparison between investors held up across almost every alternative assumption we tried. Details below.

01The person we simulated

  • Starts at age 25 with $10,000 in an S&P 500 index fund.
  • Adds $300 at the end of every month for 40 years. That's a fixed dollar amount; it does not rise with inflation or salary. Total put in: $154,000.
  • Results are shown at age 65 in today's money: final balance ÷ 1.0340 (≈ 3.26), i.e. 3% a year inflation.
  • "Typical result" in the video means the median across all runs, not the average.

02The market

We split the S&P 500 into two parts and let a cap-weighted fund hold them in proportion to their value. When one part grows faster, its share of your account grows automatically, just like a real index fund.

03The three futures

We don't say which future is more likely. Each one is run 10,000 times with fresh random ups and downs.

FutureWhat happens to the AI giants
BoomBeat the rest of the market by about 6% a year for 5 years, then normal.
Cool-offTrail the rest of the market by about 3% a year for 10 years.
PopStarting in a random month within the first 3 years, they lose 70% over 24 months while everything else loses 25%. Afterwards they trail by 2% a year for 10 years. Loosely based on 2000–02, when the Nasdaq fell about 78% and the S&P 500 about 49%.

04The three investors

InvestorRule
Held onOwns the index fund and never sells.
Panic-sellerWhen the account falls 30% below its own previous high, sells everything and moves to cash (3% / yr). Keeps adding $300 a month into cash. Buys back in, all at once, only when the fund gets back to the high it had when they sold. Repeats every time it happens.
Capped 3%Puts only 3¢ of every dollar in the AI giants and 97¢ in everything else, rebalanced monthly. This caps concentration; it is not an equal-weight fund (an equal-weight S&P 500 fund is the closest real-world idea).

Every investor in a future sees the exact same random market path, so differences come from their choices, not luck.

07What held up, and what didn't

  • Held up: the typical panic-seller fell behind in all three futures in 8 of the 9 return/volatility settings, and with 1% cash, 0.9 correlation, and deeper or shallower crashes. Panic beat holding in a minority of runs (3%–12%).
  • Depends on the rule: buying back sooner shrinks the damage a lot. With a 6-month rule the gap is close to zero. The cost of panic comes mostly from time spent out of the market during the recovery. The video says this out loud.
  • Only in our main setup: "the typical panic-seller in the best future ended up with less than the typical holder in the worst one" holds when AI-giant volatility is 25% or 30% and fails at 20%. That's why the video says "in our main run."
  • Not robust: the dollar amounts. They range from about $270K to over $1M depending on the return assumption.

08Limitations

  • The sizes of the boom, cool-off and pop are illustrative choices, not predictions.
  • Normally distributed returns understate how often extreme crashes happen.
  • No fees, taxes, employer match, or salary growth.
  • The panic rule is one simple behavior. Real people panic in many different ways.
  • Two parts of the market is a simplification of 500 companies.

10Run it yourself

You need Python 3 and numpy (pip install numpy). Save both files in one folder and run python3 run_all.py. It takes about 10 seconds and prints the same tables you see above. Random seeds are fixed (boom = 1, cool-off = 2, pop = 3). Our results were made with numpy 2.5.3.

ai401k_model.py
"""AI bubble x your 401(k): illustrative Monte Carlo. NOT a forecast.

Market = two sleeves of a cap-weighted S&P 500 fund:
  AI   : the top-10 / AI-heavy giants, 37% of the index at the start (SPY holdings, 2026-11-12 close)
  REST : everything else, 63%
A cap-weighted fund simply owns both in proportion to their current value.

Three possible AI futures (we do not assign probabilities; each is run 10,000 times):
  boom  : AI sleeve beats the rest by ~6%/yr for 5 years, then normal
  fade  : AI sleeve trails the rest by ~3%/yr for 10 years
  pop   : starting in a random month in the first 3 years, AI sleeve loses 70% over 24 months while
          the rest loses 25% (contagion); afterwards AI trails by 2%/yr for 10 years (Cisco-style)

Investors (all start at age 25 with $10,000 and add $300/month for 40 years):
  holder  : cap-weighted S&P 500 fund, never sells
  panic   : same fund; if the account falls 30% below its peak, sells everything to cash (3%/yr),
            keeps saving into cash, buys back only when the fund regains its old peak
  spread  : a fund with only 3% in the AI giants (roughly equal-weight-like), rebalanced monthly
"""
import numpy as np

MONTHS = 40 * 12
START, MONTHLY = 10_000.0, 300.0
W_AI0 = 0.37
MU_REST = 0.095            # long-run nominal annual return for the rest of the market (approx.)
SIG_REST, SIG_AI, RHO = 0.15, 0.25, 0.75
CASH = 0.03


def market_paths(n, fate, rng):
    """Monthly log returns for (AI, REST): arrays shape (n, MONTHS)."""
    dt = 1 / 12
    z1 = rng.standard_normal((n, MONTHS)); z2 = rng.standard_normal((n, MONTHS))
    e_rest = z1
    e_ai = RHO * z1 + np.sqrt(1 - RHO ** 2) * z2
    mu_rest_log = np.log1p(MU_REST) * dt
    rest = mu_rest_log + SIG_REST * np.sqrt(dt) * e_rest
    ai = mu_rest_log + SIG_AI * np.sqrt(dt) * e_ai
    m = np.arange(MONTHS)[None, :]
    if fate == "boom":
        ai = ai + np.where(m < 60, np.log1p(0.06) * dt, 0)
    elif fate == "fade":
        ai = ai + np.where(m < 120, np.log(1 - 0.03) * dt, 0)
    elif fate == "pop":
        start = rng.integers(0, 36, n)[:, None]
        crash = (m >= start) & (m < start + 24)
        after = (m >= start + 24) & (m < start + 144)
        ai = np.where(crash, np.log(0.30) / 24 + SIG_AI * np.sqrt(dt) * e_ai * 0.5, ai)
        rest = np.where(crash, np.log(0.75) / 24 + SIG_REST * np.sqrt(dt) * e_rest * 0.5, rest)
        ai = ai + np.where(after, np.log(1 - 0.02) * dt, 0)
    return ai, rest


def run(n=10_000, fate="pop", seed=0):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    ai, rest = market_paths(n, fate, rng)
    gai, grest = np.exp(ai), np.exp(rest)
    out = {}
    # index level of the cap-weighted fund (used by the panic seller's re-entry rule)
    a_val = np.full(n, W_AI0); r_val = np.full(n, 1 - W_AI0)
    idx_path = np.empty((n, MONTHS))
    for t in range(MONTHS):
        a_val = a_val * gai[:, t]; r_val = r_val * grest[:, t]
        idx_path[:, t] = a_val + r_val
    idx_ret = idx_path / np.concatenate([np.ones((n, 1)), idx_path[:, :-1]], axis=1)

    # holder
    v = np.full(n, START); low = np.full(n, START); contrib = START
    vmin_ratio = np.ones(n)
    for t in range(MONTHS):
        v = v * idx_ret[:, t] + MONTHLY
    out["holder"] = v

    # panic seller
    v = np.full(n, START); peak_acct = v.copy(); in_mkt = np.ones(n, bool)
    sold_idx_peak = np.zeros(n); idx_peak = np.ones(n); sold_ever = np.zeros(n, bool)
    months_out = np.zeros(n)
    for t in range(MONTHS):
        idx_now = idx_path[:, t]
        v = np.where(in_mkt, v * idx_ret[:, t], v * (1 + CASH) ** (1 / 12)) + MONTHLY
        idx_peak = np.maximum(idx_peak, idx_now)
        peak_acct = np.where(in_mkt, np.maximum(peak_acct, v), peak_acct)
        sell = in_mkt & (v < 0.70 * peak_acct)
        sold_idx_peak = np.where(sell, idx_peak, sold_idx_peak)
        in_mkt = in_mkt & ~sell
        sold_ever |= sell
        buy = (~in_mkt) & (idx_now >= sold_idx_peak) & ~sell
        in_mkt = in_mkt | buy
        peak_acct = np.where(buy, v, peak_acct)
        months_out += ~in_mkt
    out["panic"] = v
    out["panic_sold_share"] = sold_ever.mean()
    out["panic_months_out_median"] = np.median(months_out[sold_ever]) if sold_ever.any() else 0

    # spread (3% in AI giants, rebalanced monthly)
    w = 0.03
    sp_ret = w * gai + (1 - w) * grest
    v = np.full(n, START)
    for t in range(MONTHS):
        v = v * sp_ret[:, t] + MONTHLY
    out["spread"] = v

    # scary moment for the holder in the first 6 years: worst account value
    v = np.full(n, START); worst = np.full(n, np.inf)
    for t in range(72):
        v = v * idx_ret[:, t] + MONTHLY
        contributed = START + MONTHLY * (t + 1)
        worst = np.minimum(worst, v / contributed)
    out["worst_ratio_6y"] = worst
    return out


if __name__ == "__main__":
    contributed = START + MONTHLY * MONTHS
    print(f"total contributed: ${contributed:,.0f}")
    for fate in ("boom", "fade", "pop"):
        o = run(fate=fate, seed={"boom": 1, "fade": 2, "pop": 3}[fate])
        print(f"\n== {fate} ==")
        for k in ("holder", "panic", "spread"):
            x = o[k]
            print(f"  {k:7s} median ${np.median(x):>12,.0f}   10th pct ${np.percentile(x,10):>12,.0f}   90th ${np.percentile(x,90):>12,.0f}")
        print(f"  panic: share who ever sold {o['panic_sold_share']:.0%}, median months out {o['panic_months_out_median']:.0f}")
        print(f"  holder worst moment (first 6y), account/contributed: median {np.median(o['worst_ratio_6y']):.2f}")
        print(f"  panic vs holder (median of paired ratio): {np.median(o['panic']/o['holder']):.2f}")
        print(f"  spread vs holder (median of paired ratio): {np.median(o['spread']/o['holder']):.2f}")
run_all.py
"""Reproduce every number used in Episode 1 (and its stress tests).
Usage: python3 run_all.py      (numpy only; ~5-10 min)  -> results.json + results.md"""
import json, numpy as np, ai401k_model as M

INFL = 1.03 ** 40          # 3.262: nominal -> today's money
SEEDS = {"boom": 1, "fade": 2, "pop": 3}
BASE = dict(MU_REST=M.MU_REST, SIG_REST=M.SIG_REST, SIG_AI=M.SIG_AI, RHO=M.RHO, CASH=M.CASH, W_AI0=M.W_AI0)

def setp(**kw):
    for k, v in {**BASE, **kw}.items(): setattr(M, k, v)

def summarize(o):
    h, p, s = o["holder"] / INFL, o["panic"] / INFL, o["spread"] / INFL
    return dict(holder_median=float(np.median(h)), panic_median=float(np.median(p)), spread_median=float(np.median(s)),
                holder_p10=float(np.percentile(h, 10)), holder_p90=float(np.percentile(h, 90)),
                panic_vs_holder_paired_median=float(np.median(p / h) - 1), panic_beat_holder_share=float(np.mean(p > h)),
                panic_sold_at_least_once=float(o["panic_sold_share"]), spread_vs_holder_paired_median=float(np.median(s / h) - 1),
                holder_worst_ratio_first6y_median=float(np.median(o["worst_ratio_6y"])))

def run_set(n, **kw):
    setp(**kw)
    return {f: summarize(M.run(n=n, fate=f, seed=sd)) for f, sd in SEEDS.items()}

def panic_reentry(fate, seed, rule, n=4000):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    ai, rest = M.market_paths(n, fate, rng); gai, grest = np.exp(ai), np.exp(rest)
    a = np.full(n, M.W_AI0); r = np.full(n, 1 - M.W_AI0); idx = np.empty((n, M.MONTHS))
    for t in range(M.MONTHS):
        a *= gai[:, t]; r *= grest[:, t]; idx[:, t] = a + r
    ret = idx / np.concatenate([np.ones((n, 1)), idx[:, :-1]], axis=1)
    h = np.full(n, M.START)
    for t in range(M.MONTHS): h = h * ret[:, t] + M.MONTHLY
    v = np.full(n, M.START); peak = v.copy(); inm = np.ones(n, bool); ipk = np.ones(n); sold_pk = np.zeros(n)
    out_m = np.zeros(n); low = np.full(n, np.inf)
    for t in range(M.MONTHS):
        now = idx[:, t]
        v = np.where(inm, v * ret[:, t], v * (1 + M.CASH) ** (1 / 12)) + M.MONTHLY
        ipk = np.maximum(ipk, now); peak = np.where(inm, np.maximum(peak, v), peak)
        sell = inm & (v < 0.70 * peak)
        sold_pk = np.where(sell, ipk, sold_pk); low = np.where(sell, now, low)
        inm = inm & ~sell
        out_m = np.where(inm, 0, out_m + 1); low = np.where(~inm, np.minimum(low, now), low)
        buy = {"old_peak": now >= sold_pk, "after_12_months": out_m >= 12, "after_6_months": out_m >= 6,
               "plus20_off_low": now >= 1.2 * low}[rule] & ~inm & ~sell
        inm |= buy; peak = np.where(buy, v, peak)
    return dict(panic_vs_holder_paired_median=float(np.median(v / h) - 1), panic_beat_holder_share=float(np.mean(v > h)))

R = {"parameters": dict(BASE, START=M.START, MONTHLY=M.MONTHLY, MONTHS=M.MONTHS, inflation_annual=0.03, seeds=SEEDS,
                        n_main=10000, n_tests=4000, numpy_version=np.__version__)}
print("main run (10,000 per future)..."); R["main"] = run_set(10000)
print("stress grid..."); R["stress_grid"] = {}
for mu in (0.075, 0.095, 0.11):
    for sig in (0.20, 0.25, 0.30):
        R["stress_grid"][f"return_{mu}_aivol_{sig}"] = run_set(4000, MU_REST=mu, SIG_AI=sig)
print("extra tests..."); R["cash_1pct"] = run_set(4000, CASH=0.01); R["corr_0.9"] = run_set(4000, RHO=0.90)
print("re-entry rules..."); setp(); R["reentry"] = {rule: {f: panic_reentry(f, sd, rule) for f, sd in SEEDS.items()}
                                     for rule in ("old_peak", "plus20_off_low", "after_12_months", "after_6_months")}
json.dump(R, open("results.json", "w"), indent=1)
